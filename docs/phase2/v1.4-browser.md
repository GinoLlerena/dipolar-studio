# Phase 2 V1.4: the browser and the robot

V1.3 made fixed-geometry fits possible through the registry. The page could not reach them: its
model inspector had no `fixed` kind, it had no symmetry form, its Fit workspace showed only a note
for a fixed-geometry run, and its Structure view still described a torsion (φ = 45°, "default").
V1.4 closes that gap (`docs/phase2-proposal.md` §V1.4). Nothing in the fit changes.

**Gate (proposal):** the robot tutorial and the page give the same run, compared field by field
and fail-closed (the M3 comparison). The AI-readiness checks stay green.

## E-1 Contract 0.12.0 (breaking for fixed-geometry projects only)

- **`render.scene` and `render.describe_view`** give `phi: null` and `phi_source: null` for a
  project whose model is `fixed`: there is no torsion to show. `SceneOut.phi` becomes nullable.
- **`render.scene`** gains `symmetry`, present only for a fixed-geometry project whose
  declaration validates (E-3).
- **The new query `molecule.suggest_symmetry`** (E-5).
- **Every other project keeps the 0.10.0 shape** of `render.scene` and `render.describe_view`
  (the V1.3 old-client gate, extended to a torsion-less draft with a declaration).

## E-2 The Fit workspace without φ

For a run whose `model.kind` is `fixed` (decided by the run's own model kind, as `studio.ts`
already does):

- **No preview, no landscape, no "Profile intervals" option.** `fit.run(intervals=…)` has no
  effect for `fixed` (D-8): exact intervals are always given when supported. The option is
  replaced by that sentence.
- **The estimable-combinations panel** (`#pane-estimable`) lists the result's `components`:
  - *estimated* ones: the name, the estimate ± its standard error, and both exact Gaussian
    intervals (68.3 % and 95 %);
  - *projected* ones (rank unverified): the projection's estimate, with
    "not supported: rank unverified" in place of intervals, and the null directions in b;
  - *fixed by symmetry*: listed apart, "0 by the declaration, not estimated".
  - Each row shows its coefficient vector in b on request (`details`), as the report does.
- **The verdict:** the rank (required d, numerical, verified, the singular-value ratios), the
  adequacy (χ², N, d, ν, p, or the code that withholds it), the admissibility (status, λ_min, the
  certificate), the symmetry (group, tolerance, the largest displacement), the assumptions and
  the warnings, all as the result states them. The page computes none of them.
- **The residuals** are the result's `rows`, one per observed pair orbit, with its multiplicity.
  Selecting a row selects its coupling (the shared selection).

## E-3 The Structure view for one-ring molecules

For a fixed-geometry project (scene `symmetry` present):

- **No torsion is described** (`phi: null`): the view's label gives the point group instead.
- **The symmetry frame is drawn**: its three axes from the centroid, labelled x′, y′, z′ (the
  frame the order tensor is expressed in). The scene gives `origin` and `axes` (the polar factor
  Q's rows, in the scene's coordinates, which are the input coordinates).
- **The automatic camera looks along the symmetry axis with the least spread** of the averaged
  geometry (largest |x′_k| over atoms; ties within 1e-6 Å go to z′, then x′, then y′), from its
  positive side. A planar ring is therefore shown face-on whether its normal is z′ (D6h, D3h, Cs,
  a D2h ring declared with z normal) or x′ (a C2v ring in the y′z′ plane). The up vector is y′
  when looking along z′, and z′ otherwise. The scene gives both vectors (`camera_direction`,
  `camera_up`); the browser and the SVG renderer use them through the one projection, and the
  parity fixtures gain such cases.
- **Every member of a row's pair orbit is drawn.** The scene gives the group action as
  `images`: for each operation, the index in `atoms` of each atom's image (|G| ≤ 24 rows of at
  most 1000 indices, instead of every member of up to 20 000 orbits). The orbit of a row is
  {sorted(π_g(a), π_g(b))}. Each member is an arc with the row's coupling ID and misfit; picking
  any member selects the row, and a selected row shows all its members selected. In the browser,
  at most `MAX_DRAWN` (2000) arcs are drawn: the observed pairs of the drawn rows, then other
  copies (rows by misfit) while that budget lasts, and the copies of selected and highlighted rows
  with a budget of their own of `MAX_DRAWN` (so at most 2 × 2000 arcs in all; round 80); the view
  says how many copies it left out. The SVG renderer (`render.view_svg`) has no
  drawing limit and draws every member (round 79).
- **Face-on** holds when one declared axis is normal to the molecule's plane; a planar molecule
  declared with axes tilted to its plane is seen along the axis of least spread, obliquely.
- Other projects are drawn as before.

## E-4 The Data workspace

- **The symmetry form** (imported geometry): point group (C1, Cs, C2v, D2h, D3h, D6h), the three
  axes as rows, the tolerance, the medium (achiral, uniaxial: the only one). It shows the current
  declaration (`molecule.declarations`) with its largest displacement, declares it
  (`molecule.set_symmetry`) and clears it. Refusals come back with their codes
  (`SYMMETRY_NOT_SATISFIED`, `OBSERVATIONS_CONFLICT`, …), as for every command.
- **The model inspector** offers `fixed` ("Fixed geometry (no torsion)"): its order and frame are
  "point-group" and "symmetry", and it has no φ domain. Leaving it needs a φ domain, as before.
- **Isotopes** are shown (the mass number column, from `molecule.declarations`), not edited:
  `molecule.set_isotope` stays an API operation in V1.4.

## E-5 `molecule.suggest_symmetry`: the Cs suggestion (V1.0 design, risks)

"The Data workspace suggests Cs when every spin lies within `tolerance_a` of a plane. That is only
a suggestion: it is never certified." The page is a thin client (04, D2), so the server computes
it, and any client can read it. A query (any tier, nothing recorded):

- **The plane** is the spins' least-squares plane through their centroid: its normal is the
  eigenvector of the smallest eigenvalue of their scatter matrix. Every spin must lie within the
  tolerance (the declared one, else 0.01 Å) of it.
- **No suggestion** with fewer than three spins, when the spins are collinear (every spin within
  the tolerance of their least-squares line: no unique plane; round 79 pinned this rule, which
  replaced the scatter-root wording of the first draft), for the biphenyl template, and when a
  group other than C1 is declared.
- **The suggested declaration** is Cs with z′ the plane's normal (its largest-magnitude component
  made positive; ties to the first), x′ the input axis least aligned with the normal, made
  orthogonal to it, and y′ = z′ × x′ (right-handed). The tolerance is the one used.
- **It is not validated.** Declaring it validates it, like any declaration; the answer says so.
- **It promises no estimability** (round 79): Cs reduces the model to the in-plane block; which
  combinations of it the couplings determine is the fit's answer (one coupling of three planar
  protons gives rank 1 of 3).

## E-6 The tutorial: synthetic 1,4-dichlorobenzene

`datasets/dichlorobenzene-synthetic/` (BSD-3, generated by its own NumPy script, independent of
the engine): a regular ring in the xy plane, Cl on the x axis, Sxx = 0.22 and Syy = −0.06 in that
frame, σ = 0.3 Hz with a fixed seed; three pair orbits of two pairs each (N = 3, d = 2, ν = 1). The
couplings file names the protons H2 … H6 and writes out each orbit; imported, they are named by
atom number, so the mapping is confirmed, as in the biphenyl tutorial.

**The steps,** by the page and by the robot (`tools/robot/robot.py --scenario fixed-tutorial`,
HTTP registry calls only, in a clean `python -I` process):

1. `project.import_xyz` the geometry, named after the file;
2. `molecule.set_symmetry` D2h (the input frame, 0.01 Å);
3. `model.set` fixed;
4. `couplings.reconcile`, confirm the mapping from the XYZ comment line's atom names, check the
   diff, `couplings.import` that mapping;
5. `fit.run`, `job.wait`, `run.get`;
6. select the worst row (largest |residual/σ|), render the view (SVG), `project.export`.

**The comparison** (`web/e2e/fixed-tutorial.spec.ts`, on Chromium, Firefox and WebKit): the
science hash, the exported project, the selection, and the whole run read with `run.get` by each
side's own credential, with `resultDifferences` extended to `FixedRunOut`: every number of a fixed
result has an explicit rule (frozen tolerances in the dataset's `expected/spec.json`), and any
other difference fails.
