## 2. The selection protocol

A dataset is **eligible for V1** when all of these hold:

1. **The molecule is in V1's scope:** rigid, with a supported point group (C1, Cs, C2v, D2h, D3h
   or D6h).
2. **The medium is in scope:** an achiral, uniaxial nematic or smectic A.
3. **The observables:** ¹H–¹H **dipolar** couplings D, tabulated numerically.
   - Totals T = J + 2D are not eligible unless the paper separates D under a stated convention.
   - A coupling involving another nucleus is V4.
4. **Each value has an uncertainty,** and its provenance (the spectral fit's statistical error,
   or other) is stated.
5. **The conditions are stated:** solvent, temperature, concentration, and the sample (which
   solutes share it).
6. **The source.** A primary source, or a copy the authors published. A table reproduced in
   another paper (a secondary source) is used only as a cross-check.
7. **Over-determination.** At least one condition has more coupling classes than order
   parameters (ν ≥ 1 with fixed geometry); ν ≥ 2 is preferred.
8. **Rights are known.** At least: may it be stored for validation, and may it be
   redistributed?

**What each dataset's record must give** (its README, in V0.2):
- the sign and label conventions;
- the published frame;
- the geometry the authors used and its source;
- whether vibrational corrections were applied;
- the σ provenance, and whether the couplings come from one joint spectral fit (correlated);
- the grouping of related observations (sample, run, paper series).

### What each selected dataset can and cannot show

- **Benzene (Taggar et al.).**
  - **The published S_zz** (Tables 1–2 of the paper) comes from SHAPE with the geometry of
    Diehl & Niederberger, *J. Magn. Reson.* 9, 495 (1973), which we have not obtained. Their
    frame has z normal to the ring and x along a C–X bond. With one independent order parameter,
    S_zz follows from D12 and the ortho H–H distance alone.
  - **A consistency check:** the first sample's D12 = −1024.174 Hz and S_zz = −0.25942 imply an
    ortho distance of 2.478 Å, which is right for benzene. So **agreeing with the published S_zz
    reproduces their analysis; it is not independent support.**
  - **The independent content is the coupling ratios.** For a rigid regular hexagon,
    D13/D12 = 3^(−3/2) = 0.19245 and D14/D12 = 1/8 = 0.125. The first sample gives 0.19404
    and 0.12659, deviating by 0.8 % and 1.3 %: the known effect of vibrational averaging,
    which a rigid fixed-geometry model ignores.
  - **The expected result** with σ of 0.015–0.04 Hz is a rigid fit **flagged inadequate**,
    honestly. Taggar et al. themselves note that choices of bond length move order parameters
    by about 1 %, and that vibrations and reorientation–vibration effects are neglected.
- **ortho-Dichlorobenzene (Chandrakumar & Burnell).**
  - With 4 classes, 2 order parameters, ν = 2, and 26 conditions, it is the best-determined
    rigid proton-only dataset found.
  - The geometry they used is not given in the pages read. It is needed for a comparison with
    their order parameters, and is to be found in V0.2 (their ref. 14 gives the J values).
- **The plan's candidates that fail.**
  - Fluorobenzene and 1,4-dichlorobenzene: no coupling table (Yethiraj et al. 2004).
  - 1,3,5-trichlorobenzene: ν = 0.
  - 4,4′-d₂-biphenyl: not V1.

### σ and correlations

Every selected σ is a statistical error from a joint spectral fit (LEQUOR). The couplings of
one sample are therefore correlated, and no covariance is published. Per the proposal:
- the comparison says the σ are treated as independent;
- the adequacy test is conditional on that assumption;
- the README states it.

These σ are also known to be far smaller than the model's misfit, as with 8CB.

## 4. Grouping and holdouts (the custodian rules)

- **Groups.** Each paper is one group. Within Taggar et al., the mixed-solute samples share a
  tube with other solutes, so each run is a subgroup.
- **Exposure.** During this survey the author (the AI agent writing this page) **saw the values
  of every selected dataset**: the benzene, tcb and tbb tables, and the ortho-dichlorobenzene
  Tables I–II. The exposure log records this (2026-11-18). **No independent holdout remains
  among these sources.**
  - Both selected datasets are therefore **development data**.
  - Their comparisons are labelled "no independent holdout".
- **For a true holdout,** the owner (the custodian) would choose and keep a further dataset that
  the author does not see until V1's model and criteria are frozen (V0.3). The proposal allows
  reporting that none exists, so this is the owner's choice, not a gate.

## 5. Rights

- **Taggar et al., Supporting Information: CC BY-NC 4.0.** It may be stored and redistributed
  for non-commercial use, with attribution. Under decision 3's default it is still kept for
  validation only, outside releases. The licence would allow more if the owner wanted.
- **Chandrakumar & Burnell (1993): no licence stated.** It is publicly hosted by ISMAR. It is
  kept as **validation-only, removable on request, not redistributed**: the 8CB policy.
- **Published values used as facts in a comparison** (the S_zz in Taggar et al.'s Tables 1–2)
  are cited, not copied as a dataset.

## 6. What V0.2 needs from the owner

- **Benzene needs no transcription.** The Supporting Information is a plain-text file, parsed
  mechanically and checked by its SHA-256.
- **ortho-Dichlorobenzene needs transcription** from the rendered pages: 2 tables × 13 rows × 4
  couplings = 104 values with σ. This is **decision 4** (the owner enters the values and an AI
  cross-checks them, or two independent AI transcriptions are compared mechanically and the
  owner spot-checks). It is still open.
- **The geometries** (Diehl & Niederberger 1973 for benzene; Chandrakumar & Burnell's source for
  ortho-dichlorobenzene) may need the owner's library access (decision 5).
