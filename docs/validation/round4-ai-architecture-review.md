# Round 4: AI-ready architecture and biphenyl validation

Reviewed 2026-11-07: plan v0.4, architecture v0.1 (D13–D20), both datasets, and rounds 1–3.

**Verdict: NOT READY to approve v0.4 + 04 as the Phase-1 execution baseline.** The direction is sound, and M0 exploration can proceed. The new baseline contains a false scientific equivalence, an acceptance criterion its own fixture fails, and unresolved state/security contracts that affect implementation boundaries. These require explicit corrections before baseline approval.

**Owner requirements:** (1) the pure scientific core satisfies headless calculation by design; (2) a shared application registry can serve calculations and view management equally; (3) postponing the agent and MCP implementation is appropriate. However, “Phase 2 only adds an adapter and client” is not established until revision ownership, replay, policy, rendering and session attachment are specified. Otherwise those become refactors.

**Method/limits:** inspected documents and scripts; reran generators/fits in temporary copies, preserving repository inputs; performed independent NumPy geometry/tensor checks. The supplied text extraction was absent. Downloaded the [Di Pietro thesis](https://lisa.unical.it/bitstreams/1deb8605-44d0-4c62-9a8f-d4f7eb6cafe1/download), extracted and visually inspected Tables 3.1/3.2 and Figure 3.6. Consulted official MCP specifications. No application exists here to validate runtime controls, latency or rendering parity. Only this report was written in the project.

## Dataset findings

- **Transcription passes:** all 12 experimental values **and uncertainties** at each of 0.930 and 0.980 match Table 3.1, printed pp. 167–168/PDF pp. 179–180. All 12 also match at spot-check temperatures 0.880 and 0.990; experimental and simulated columns are correctly distinguished.
- **Numbering passes:** thesis `(1,1′; 2,2′; 5)` maps to `(H2,H6; H3,H5; H4)`; `(3,3′; 4,4′; 6)` maps to `(H2′,H6′; H3′,H5′; H4′)`. The figure places 1 and 3 on the same side. The 12 CSV representative pairs and their stated equalities are consistent with this map and the jump average.
- **Fits reproduce:** synthetic φ̂=36.985°, Szz=0.25004, Δ=0.03053, χ²=12.430, ν=11, inner rank=2. Experimental `(φ,Szz,Δ,RMS Hz)` is `(32.95,0.4308,0.1852,5.24)` at 0.930 and `(33.05,0.3248,0.1531,3.89)` at 0.980. Table 3.2's reference numbers also match. Agreement of order parameters within 1–2% is descriptive, not independent validation of the different geometry/distribution model. The proposed explanations for the roughly 2.4° angle offset are plausible hypotheses, not demonstrated attribution.
- **Physics substantially passes:** the stated bisector frame, traceless diagonal contraction and negative angular-γ prefactor give KHH=−120120.161 Hz Å³. There are 14 coefficient classes covering all 45 pairs for this regular-ring fixture. Some equality is geometric coincidence, notably H2–H6 versus H3–H5, rather than a general D2 symmetry identity. Explicitly rotating four conformers and their acute-frame tensors agrees with simultaneous ring-B permutation averaging within 1.6×10⁻¹² Hz at φ=37°.

## Required changes, ranked

1. **Correct the rigid equivalence and generalize the jump contract (D12/D19; datasets).** At fixed labels, `(±φ,Δ) ≡ (180±φ,−Δ)` is false. With Szz=0.25, H2–H2′ predicts −1124.298342 Hz at `(37°,0.03)` and +69.412618 Hz at `(143°,−0.03)`. The latter equals the original H2–H6′ prediction: the transformation also permutes ring-B labels. State that permutation, its assignment assumptions, and the frame-dependent meaning of Δ. The experimental shortcut swaps only its second endpoint; this works for the supplied representatives but maps H2′–H6′ onto a zero-length vector. Permute **both endpoints simultaneously**, or explicitly restrict this script to its representative list. Gate the engine on all 45 unordered pairs, reversed pairs, explicit four-conformer averaging, and the ideal 90° endpoint. Do not infer a unique equal-population four-site mechanism from observed equalities alone.

2. **Repair M1's scientific acceptance criteria.** The synthetic truth 37° lies outside its reported 68% profile interval `[36.976°,36.994°]`. That is possible under correct statistical coverage; the plan's literal “truth recovered within its interval” gate nevertheless fails. Use noiseless recovery, fixed noisy numerical regression, and separately calibrated ensemble coverage; do not choose seeds to force coverage. The experimental reference is **unweighted**, whereas D3 defaults to spectral-σ weighting. Declare a separate unweighted regression configuration and test both temperatures, Δ, RMS and prediction vectors, with numerical tolerances. Weighted 0.980 instead gives approximately φ=33.10°, Δ=0.15221. Rounded grid outputs are regression targets, not a scientific gold standard. Preserve spectral uncertainties; report gross inadequacy under that likelihood rather than calling χ² intrinsically meaningless. Reconcile §6's 15-observation benchmark with the 14-row synthetic fixture.

3. **Separate revision domains and define atomic concurrency/jobs (D14/D18).** Keep journal sequence, science revision/hash, workspace revision and job status distinct. Camera moves, proposals and job progress must not stale fits or invalidate science writes. Require the relevant expected revision for state-dependent mutations; an omitted check must not silently bypass concurrency. Check-and-commit must be atomic. Return the current revision and a bounded conflict description, with no mutation on conflict. Add request deduplication for retries after lost responses. Jobs must capture immutable scientific inputs at submission, publish against that input identity, retain stale results without replacing newer active state, and define cancellation/completion races. Bounded waits and CPU isolation are good choices.

4. **Make replay and undo executable contracts (D14/D15).** An `args_hash` plus inverse is insufficient to reconstruct forward changes, imports or generated IDs. Journal versioned forward payloads or immutable content references, command IDs, transaction boundaries and snapshot lineage, with crash-safe commit/recovery rules. Replay applies recorded events rather than rerunning jobs or external I/O. Keep the separate view undo stack, but explicitly handle references to deleted objects and obsolete runs. “Undo per actor” cannot blindly apply old inverses over another actor's dependent edits: initially support global chronological science undo, or reject selective undo unless dependency checks pass. Undo creates a new audited command and never decreases revisions; exported files and completed jobs are not magically undone.

5. **Specify trusted policy and proposal transactions (D17).** Derive identity and capabilities from authenticated credentials; never trust an `actor` or tier supplied in tool arguments. Define an effect/capability matrix: `read` currently includes view writes, while compute, import, export and cancellation remain ambiguous. Reserve approval for an authorized human principal. Proposals need base revisions, dependency-aware partial acceptance, atomic validation, expiry/conflict behavior and preservation of both proposer and approver. Dry runs may read/validate bounded inputs but must not write files, launch jobs or consume scientific revisions; cost is an estimate. Escaping labels and marking strings as data do **not** prevent prompt injection. Enforce capabilities independently of model text. Replace D19's “no room to invent” guarantee with traceability requirements and evaluation.

6. **Define achievable headless perception parity (D15/D16).** A second orthographic renderer cannot reproduce a browser's unrestricted perspective camera. Either constrain beta projection or implement the same projection/viewport/clipping conventions. Share a renderer-independent scene description containing stable IDs, coordinates, visibility, highlights, annotations and styles. Compare that structure plus projection and depth behavior; visible/highlighted-ID agreement alone misses materially different images. `describe_view` must use the active run and state snapshot and expose staleness. Define which shared or detached view a tool targets. Keep transient drag feedback local and commit coalesced semantic view changes. Defer detached collaborative views if unnecessary for beta.

7. **Tighten registry generation, UI coverage and D20 compatibility.** One typed dispatcher is sufficient; generators should wrap its policy/validation rather than replicate them. Validate JSON-serializable units, IDs, errors and output schemas in the M0 spike. “No private UI API” should cover all domain capabilities while explicitly allowing authentication, uploads/artifact bytes and event transport. An endpoint scan cannot detect frontend fitting, reconciliation or selection logic: add dependency-boundary checks and behavioral parity tests. D20 must treat new required inputs, narrowed ranges, changed defaults/units/effects and changed error semantics as potentially breaking. Specify unknown-field/enum handling, removal only at the appropriate major boundary, and separate project/event schema migrations. A schema snapshot alone misses semantic changes; retain behavioral compatibility fixtures.

8. **Re-sequence and reduce the solo roadmap; strengthen 04 §7.** Build one vertical registry/session/view workflow in M0 before multiplying adapters and “~40” operations. Keep a single shared view and conservative undo; defer arbitrary partial proposals and broad adapter automation unless the spike justifies them. Include required safety semantics in the reduced scope. The checklist is useful but insufficient: add clean-process execution without frontend/browser dependencies, policy-denial tests, stale proposal rejection, view changes during running fits, disconnect/retry deduplication, journal crash recovery and cross-actor undo conflicts. Define “equal results” with quantity-specific tolerances and “within 200 ms” on named hardware. Move minimum A4 safety gates before exposing A3 to users. Keep the holdout independent and M-L gated; remove obsolete desktop/Tauri packaging requirements from the browser baseline.

9. **Specify the MCP adapter's session topology and protocol mapping (A1).** A locally launched stdio server must attach to the **same running session** as the browser, rather than create a second in-memory project. Choose and document that authenticated attachment now. MCP is a suitable optional adapter, not a guarantee that every model/client supports identical features. Pin a protocol revision and SDK. Map operations to schema-described [tools](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/latest/server/tools), including structured outputs, error mapping and behavior annotations; annotations are not authorization. Expose URI-addressed snapshots/artifacts as [resources](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/latest/server/resources), with optional subscription support and polling fallback. D18's application JobId is not automatically an MCP task or resource. Use [stdio or Streamable HTTP](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/latest/basic/transports); ordinary REST/WebSocket events are not themselves MCP. Retain explicit job cancellation independently of cancellation of a transport request.

10. **Resolve public-distribution provenance before publishing the corpus.** Keep BSD-3 for owned software, and document dataset-specific rights separately. “Rights review pending before redistribution beyond this repository” overlooks the owner's public-repository decision. Record the basis for publishing the transcribed measurements now; open PDF access and citation alone do not establish a redistribution licence. Add the dataset reference to machine-readable citation metadata, preserve source/table/page/column provenance and a source checksum, and distinguish the 2013 experimental data from the separate 2010 geometry reference. Do not redistribute thesis figures or the PDF without a supported basis. D9 should record the owner's BSD decision while retaining ownership/dependency checks.
